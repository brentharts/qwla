# Roadmap: from a Spectre qubit network toward a quantum-gravity theory

This is a plan, not a result. Nothing in this repository or in its companion papers is a theory of quantum gravity, and the stages below are ordered by what can be tested next, not by how close the goal is. Each stage has a deliverable and a pass/fail test, so that a stage can be abandoned honestly.

Companion repositories: `spectre-monotile-py` (exact tilings, flips, pair Hamiltonian; paper `quantgravedit.tex`, long draft `quantgrav.tex`), `wada-coudene` (window and flip engine), `spectre` (Hat and Spectre source, `SPECTRE_DIR`).

Labels used throughout: **Exact** (proved or finite exact computation), **Counted/Measured** (numerical, one setting), **Negative** (a natural expectation refuted), **Hypothesis** (untested).

## 1. What a theory has to supply

A discrete approach to quantum gravity needs, at minimum, the following. The right-hand columns say where the Spectre programme stands.

| # | Requirement | Status in this programme | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | A background-independent, local move set | Phason flips are clusters of about ten tiles at uniform density, not single moves; whether clusters are valid tilings, and whether they are Pachner-type moves, is undecided | Measured; open |
| R2 | A Hilbert space and a Hamiltonian | One qubit per bump-and-dent pair, `H_eff = -J sum tau^x - sum K_AB tau^z tau^z`, dipolar frustrated couplings; the unique classical ground state is known exactly to 28 pairs | Exact |
| R3 | Geometry that is dynamical, not fixed | The contact graph changes in 75% of tile-changing flips; the pair count changes while the area ledger is kept. No rule yet turns this into a unitary or a quantum channel | Measured; open |
| R4 | A semiclassical limit (continuum, isotropy, diffeomorphism-like symmetry) | The inflation `lambda^2 = 4 + sqrt(15)` gives a scale ladder and the pair-rule table closes at 53 types from level 4; no continuum limit of the Hamiltonian is defined | Exact for the rule; open for the limit |
| R5 | Sources and a Newtonian limit | None: the tile as it stands has no monopole source, no horizon and no light bending of the right sign | Negative |
| R6 | An arrow or an ordering of time | None from flips (the relation is symmetric); Hamiltonians are real so entropy growth is time-symmetric about the initial state | Negative |
| R7 | Observables that look gravitational: area laws, entanglement geometry, error correction | Erasure is unambiguous (hole of 300 tiles has one refilling), which is the local-recoverability half of the Li-Boyle code condition; no entropy or area-law measurement on the network yet | Exact for refilling; open |
| R8 | Matter and gauge fields | A Z2 gauge form on the patch complex and an exact flux law exist; the lifted tetrad flux is zero | Exact; no dynamics |

The programme is strongest at R2 and R7 (substrate, Hamiltonian, recoverability) and weakest at R4 to R6. The roadmap therefore starts by making the simulator match the exact structure, and only then asks the gravitational questions.

## 2. Stages

### Stage 0: make this repository say what it does (done)
Problems found by running the first version, and what was done (all checked by `--tests-only`):

- *Tile counts.* The tiers had 8, 63 and 496 tiles instead of 9, 71, 559, because a Mystic (two tiles) was counted as one object. The exact builder gives 9, 71, 559, 4401 for singles and 8, 62, 488, 3842 for Mystics.
- *Substitution.* Children were placed by sector offsets, so the patches were not tilings. The patches are now the exact edge-matched tilings of `spectre_exact.py` (integer `Z[zeta_12]`), tested for the tile shape and area, for no overlap, and for edge matching.
- *Qubits.* Qubits now sit on bump-and-dent pairs (6, 82, 804 at generations 2, 3, 4), not on 4 hub vertices. The 9-tile patch has no pair, so the tiers are generations 2, 3, 4. The number of qubits per tier is still a budget (the central pairs), because the joint state is dense.
- *Feedback.* It was inert: a Hadamard on every qubit gives `<Z> = 0` for all later RZ and CZ gates, and `curve_strength` cannot change the area of a point-symmetric Bezier tile. The state now starts in `|0..0>`, evolves under the pair Hamiltonian (Trotter steps tested against exact diagonalisation), and `<Z_p>` moves the flat vertex, changing the two areas by `+-a h <Z_p>` with the pair area conserved.
- *Joint state.* The old code projected the joint state onto invented marginal amplitudes; one joint `State` is now kept and read directly.
- *Docs.* The README module table named files that do not exist; it now matches the files.

Not done: `qubits_per_tier` is small (4 by default), so most pairs carry no qubit; the tiers differ only in how many pairs exist, and the three tiers' central pairs are locally alike, so they give similar `<Z>`.

### Stage 1: an exact substrate (mostly done)
Done: exact edge-matched tilings, qubits on pairs, the area ledger in the geometry, and pair counts 6, 82, 804, 7012 reproduced for generations 2 to 4 (the generation 5 count and the 53-type pair-rule table are not yet recovered inside this repository; they are in `spectre-monotile-py`).
Pass: a test recovering the pair-rule table from the patch, and pair counts to generation 5.

### Stage 2: dynamics of the qubits (done for 6 to 16 pairs; J and kappa from the flow, as definitions)
Load the dipolar pair Hamiltonian into the QWLA `State` simulator for small clusters (6 to 14 pairs, matrix-free) and compare with the exact diagonalisation in `research/toymodel_v6` and `v10`. Measure the ground-state concurrence, CHSH of reduced pairs, and the gap along the Spectre-to-Hat parameter.
Finding so far (`figures/fig_dynamics.png`): with the geometric dipole couplings (`|W| <= 0.06` in units of `(a h)^2` for the central pairs) and the former defaults `J = 1`, `kappa = 0.09`, the couplings were about 1% of the tunnelling and the pairs nearly independent; the Trotter evolution agrees with exact diagonalisation to 1e-4 at `J = 0.05`, `kappa = 1` and to 1.4e-4 at the flow parameters below. The network now defaults to the Spectre-to-Hat flow (below), where the frustration matters.
Pass: agreement with the exact values (cat ground state at zero tunnelling; largest pairwise concurrence at an intermediate `tau`). A mismatch is a bug in one of the two programs.

**Done in this repository (`pair_hamiltonian.py`, `figures/fig_stage2_tau_scan.png`; **Exact** or **Counted**, central pairs of generation 3, 6 to 16 pairs):**
- The matrix-free `State` evolution agrees with exact evolution; second-order (Strang) steps converge as `dt^2` in the state vector, first-order as `dt`. The agreement is checked at `kappa = 20`, where `kappa W` is near 1; at the default scale the two terms nearly commute and the check is trivial.
- Zero tunnelling: the unique classical ground state is a doublet (`s`, `-s`), the ground state is the even cat state, its entropy across any cut is exactly 1 bit, and the cat-doublet splitting falls from 1.6e-4 (6 pairs) to 2e-13 (16 pairs) at `tau = 0.1`. The parity-sector solver separates this doublet exactly; a plain Lanczos solver returns an arbitrary mix of the two states, which would make every entanglement measure depend on solver noise.
- Intermediate `tau`: the largest pairwise concurrence peaks at `tau` of 0.4 to 1.2 for every size, **but it does not grow with size** (0.38, 0.52, 0.65, 0.51, 0.44, 0.35 at 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16 pairs) and the CHSH value above 2 (up to 2.33 at 10 pairs) is gone at 16. The reading is a finite-size effect plus monogamy as the cluster grows. It is **not** evidence of a phase or of a persistent Bell-violating network. The pair of 16 comes from generation 4, the others from generation 3, so the sizes are not a pure scan.
- The gap along the Spectre-to-Hat parameter is **not done** (exact patches need `a = b = 1`).

**`J`, `kappa` and `h` from the flow (done as definitions; a derivation is still open).** The scan shows that the model changes character at `tau = J / (kappa w)` of about 0.3 to 1.2, while the former default `J = 1`, `kappa = 0.09` was `tau` of 190 to 260 across the three tiers (a product state). `toymodel_v6` (spectre-monotile-py) defines a flow from the Spectre to the Hat: `h(tau) = tan(60 degrees (1 - tau))`, tunnelling `J = exp(-h^2 / 2)` and coupling `kappa = kappa_norm h^2`, with `kappa_norm` fixing the strongest coupling of generation 2 at the Spectre end to 1. `pair_hamiltonian.flow_parameters` implements it and `TriSpectreNetwork` now defaults to it at the Spectre end (and for `flow_tau > 0` the tiles and couplings are recomputed at `b(tau)` from the exact vertices, `x_b = (p + q w) + b z (r + s w)`, which matches `toymodel_v6`'s independent patch code to 1e-14 in the couplings and to 6 printed digits in concurrence, CHSH and gap at seven flow times; the rescaled `flow_scan` is kept as a labelled approximation, off by at most 0.014 in concurrence) (`h = sqrt(3)`, `J = 0.223`, `kappa = 17.4`, `tau` of 0.22 to 0.30 per tier). Checks: `kappa_norm`, `tau` of 0.244 and the largest concurrence 0.407 of 10 central pairs reproduce `toymodel_v6` (independent patch code); along the flow `tau` rises from 0.24 to infinity, so the flow crosses the window, and the concurrence peaks at flow time 0.05 (`tau` of 0.42). **Limits:** the functional forms of `h(tau)`, the overlap and `kappa` are `toymodel_v6`'s Definitions, so this fixes `J / kappa` by a stated rule, not by a derivation; and `h = sqrt(3)` is safe for `curve_strength` 0.5 but crosses at 0.8.

### Stage 3: flips as dynamics
A flip changes which tiles touch, so the Hilbert space (the set of pairs) changes. Define one rule for how a flip acts on the qubits: pairs that persist keep their state, pairs that are created or destroyed are initialised or traced out, and the area ledger and the pair-parity flux are conserved. Then test conservation, reversibility (the flip relation is symmetric, so the rule should be) and whether the resulting channel is unitary on a fixed extended space.
Prerequisite from `spectre-monotile-py`: decide whether flip clusters are tilings (exact refilling of the holes of valid flips).
Pass: a flip channel that conserves the ledger and is its own inverse in the reversed step; fail: any rule that needs the pair count to be conserved (it is not).

### Stage 4: geometry from entanglement (first measurement; inconclusive; QMC attempted at 82 and 804 pairs)
Define a distance from the state, for example from mutual information between pairs, and compare it with the contact-graph distance and the physical distance. Measure the entanglement entropy of a region against its boundary length on levels 3 and 4.
Pass: a stable scaling law (area law, or a measured exponent) and a distance that correlates with the contact graph. A volume law or no correlation closes the idea.

**QMC beyond exact diagonalisation (`qmc_pairs.py`, `qmc_stage4.py`; **Counted** for the validation, negative for the large sizes).** The Hamiltonian is stoquastic, so a path integral has no sign problem: discrete Trotter slices (`dtau = 0.1`), single-spin, segment and whole-world-line flips, and parallel tempering in `beta` (replicas with smaller `dtau` at fixed slice number, swaps exact). *Validated* against exact diagonalisation (thermal, flow time 0.05, `beta` 25 to 40): 3, 6, 8 pairs agree within noise, and the tempered sampler gives energy within 0.015 and `<Z_i Z_j>` within 0.02 to 0.05 (maximum over pairs) at 10, 12 and 16 pairs after 80 000 sweeps (a plain chain at 10 pairs needs 100 000 for 0.08; without tempering shorter runs are stuck in different classical basins, errors up to 0.9). A residual energy offset of 0.01 to 0.015 (0.1%) at `dtau = 0.1` is Trotter error, not checked against `dtau -> 0` at 16 pairs. **Not converged at the sizes asked for:** at 82 pairs (`beta` 25) two chains agree in energy per pair (-0.6976 and -0.6977, 30 000 sweeps, 8 replicas; kept in `qmc_results/r8`) but not in correlations (rms difference of `<Z Z>` 0.10, maximum 0.41, correlation 0.99; a 14-replica run with swap rate 0.36 drifts in the energy between halves and does not settle in 20 000 sweeps); at 804 pairs (`beta` 12.5, 2400 sweeps) the two chains' energies per pair differ (-0.7095, -0.7036, both still falling), swap rate is 0.00, and the correlations are uncorrelated between chains (rank correlation 0.07, differences up to 1.96). The system is a frustrated, glassy classical landscape (mixed-sign `r^-3` couplings), so sampling its ground state needs far more replicas and sweeps than these runs. **No entanglement measure comes from this**: the path integral gives a thermal state and Z-basis correlators; the doublet mixture is not the even ground state, and a Renyi-2 estimate (replica ratio trick) is not implemented. So Stage 4 at 82 and 804 pairs is *not* answered; the 12 to 20 pair exact results above remain the evidence.

**First measurement (`entanglement_geometry.py`, `figures/fig_stage4_geometry.png`; **Counted**, exact ground states of 12 to 20 central pairs of generation 4). Inconclusive, not closed, not passed:**
- *Distance.* Mutual information between pairs falls with the distance (Spearman -0.62 to -0.67 at `tau = 1` for 12 to 20 pairs; about -0.85 in the paramagnet at `tau = 3`) and with the tile-contact-graph distance by almost the same amount. A shuffled-position null gives 0.00 +- 0.13, so the correlation is real (4.6 to 6.4 standard deviations at `tau = 1`, up to 8.4 at `tau = 3`).
- *What the correlation means.* The couplings `W` are computed from the same positions, so some geometry is put in by hand. `abs(W)` ranks with `I` at 0.6 to 0.9. Controlling for it leaves a partial rho of -0.32 to -0.40 at `tau = 1` (2.5 to 4 sd from the null, stable from 12 to 20 pairs). This is the extra reach of correlations that travel through intermediate pairs, which any many-body state has. It is **not** evidence that space emerges; it shows that a distance read from `I` is a smoothed function of `W`.
- *No distance in the ordered phase.* At `tau = 0.1` the ground state is the cat state: `I` averages 0.95 to 0.98 bit over the pairs of pairs (maximum 0.998), the fitted entropy constant is 0.96 to 0.99 bit (the GHZ bit), and the rank correlation with distance is weak (-0.28 to -0.45, on differences of a few percent of a bit). The distance signal becomes strong only once the cat state has melted.
- *Area against volume.* For random regions at `tau = 1`, the entropy follows the boundary (Delaunay links of the pair positions cut) better than the volume: rms residual 0.162 against 0.213 (12), 0.171 against 0.217 (14), 0.192 against 0.230 (16), 0.215 against 0.253 (18), 0.227 against 0.291 (20) bits, and the standardised coefficient on the boundary is 2 to 4 times that on the volume. But the boundary and the volume still correlate at 0.68 to 0.83, adding the volume barely improves the fit, and regions never exceed 10 qubits. A volume law is not seen; an area law is **not established**.
- *What would decide it.* The Hamiltonian is stoquastic (every off-diagonal element in the Z basis is `-J`), so it has no sign problem: quantum Monte Carlo can reach the 82 and 804 pairs of generations 3 and 4, and the second Renyi entropy is accessible by replica methods. The same Monte Carlo would also settle the open item "converge the dipole annealing for 82 and 804 pairs".

### Stage 4b: relative entropy, from `NariaiRelativeEntropy` (**Counted**; negative for a direct transplant)
The sibling fork `brentharts/NariaiRelativeEntropy` derives the area law from the Araki-Umegaki relative entropy `S(rho || sigma)` of a perturbed state against a reference (`S_rel = 2 pi dQ`, the first law of the modular Hamiltonian, on the compact horizon of `dS2 x S2`). Three things carry over and are implemented in `relative_entropy.py`: (i) the relative entropy of regions of the flow ground state, `S(rho_A(tau + delta) || rho_A(tau))`, computed from eigendecompositions with the fork's two numerical traps handled (a state with weight where the reference has none is `inf`, never a clipped log; eigenvalues below the noise floor are dropped before `x ln x`); (ii) the first law `S_rel = Delta<K> - Delta S` with `K = -ln sigma`, checked to 1e-10 on random mixed states together with positivity and Pinsker; (iii) monotonicity under restriction, `S_A <= S_B` for `A` in `B`, checked on random pure states and on the flow ground states.
**Results (flow time 0.05, `delta` = 0.004, 8 to 14 central pairs of generation 3; compact and random regions):** the relative entropy of a region follows its *volume*, not its boundary: volume-only fit rms 0.0003 to 0.0011 nats against 0.0007 to 0.0015 for the Delaunay boundary alone, and with both regressors the boundary coefficient is zero or slightly negative (-0.0001 to -0.0003 against +0.0006 to +0.0013 for the volume); the response to a uniform change of couplings is extensive, so this is the expected reading and not a failure of the area law of the entropy itself. The second-order structure is confirmed (`S_rel` falls by 3.5 to 4.5 when `delta` halves, the Fisher-metric scaling) while the area ledger of the same tiles, `2 m dA(1, b)`, changes at first order, so the ratio `S_rel / dA` is not a constant: for a three-pair region it is 1.7e-2, 7.9e-3, 3.9e-3 at `delta` 0.008, 0.004, 0.002. **There is therefore no `dA = 4 G S_rel` in this model at the level of the area ledger.** The Nariai relation compares two quantities that are both quadratic in the field amplitude; here the pair's area change is linear in the bit (`+- h z`) and neutral for a pair (the two tiles change by opposite amounts), which is the structure of the fork's "zero-sum reallocation" theorem: negativity windows that integrate to zero against the modular weight. That is a conceptual match with Stage 6's negative result (a neutral dipole cannot source net area), not a derivation. **Cross-check with `brentharts/CICY`:** `pyCICY.theories.spectre` re-derives the Spectre census and areas in exact `Q(sqrt15)`, `Q(sqrt3)`; `quantumspectremodel` agrees: the swapped class of tiles at `b != 1` is the Mystic, its fraction 496/4401 matches `(5 - sqrt15)/10` to 4e-9 and `A(1, sqrt3) = 8 sqrt3`, `A(sqrt3, 1) = 10 sqrt3` (test `test_swapped_class_is_the_mystic_...`). **Not transferred:** the continuum modular theory (type III algebras, the `1/4` as a definition of `G_N`) and the Lean layer have no counterpart for 14 qubits.

### Stage 4c: can any area change in the model match a relative entropy? (**Counted**; negative)
The follow-up asked for a non-neutral area change that is quadratic in the amplitude of an excitation, the P8-displacement version closest to Nariai's setup (`relative_entropy.py`, 8 qubits of generation 3, flow time 0.05). Excitations are `exp(-i alpha sum c_q Y_q)` on a region `A` (a local unitary, so `S(rho_A)` is unchanged and `S_rel = Delta<K_A>` exactly, checked to 1e-9). What the model offers as area observables, read from the actual tiles (`geometry_area_change`, agreement 1e-9): the bump tile `A0 + h Z` and its partner `A0 - h Z` change by `+- h Delta<Z>`, **linear in the state amplitude**; their sum, the pair ledger, does not change at all; and the squared deviation `Q_A = (h sum Z)^2` is the only quadratic one. Results: (1) `S_rel` and `Delta Q_A` scale as `alpha^2` (ratios 4.0 on halving `alpha`) but the bump-tile area scales as `alpha`, so no linear area change can be proportional to `S_rel`; (2) the ratios `S_rel / Delta Q_A` are constant in `alpha` for a given region and generator but vary by region (0.51 to 0.98 for four regions of 2 to 4 pairs, with one generator each) and, across 30 random generators on one three-pair region, `S_rel / Delta Q_A` has mean 0.8 and standard deviation 14.6 (range -50 to +49): `Delta Q_A` is sign-indefinite while `S_rel >= 0`, which is the opposite of an area that grows only under positive relative entropy; `S_rel / (Delta A_bump)^2` ranges from 2.7 to 3500. So no `G_N`-like constant relates an area observable of the model to the relative entropy. (3) Why Nariai's relation works and this one does not: there `K_A` is the boost energy (Bisognano-Wichmann: the modular Hamiltonian of a wedge is a local energy weighted by position) and `S_rel = beta Delta E`; here `K_A = -ln rho_A` is **not** proportional to the region's Hamiltonian `H_A`: the best single `beta` leaves a relative residual of 0.16 (2 pairs), 0.33 (3), 0.29 (3, another region) and 0.49 (4); allowing a free weight on each term of `H_A` still leaves 0.3% (2 pairs) to 31% (4 pairs), and the weights are position dependent (`beta_eff` 3.5 to 6.5), with a few negative `ZZ` weights. This is the stage's answer: the model has the structure of the fork's zero-sum theorem (neutral ledger) but not the relation to energy that makes `S_rel` a flux integral. Not tested: mixed states / thermal references (where `S_rel = beta Delta E` is exact for unitary perturbations of the global Gibbs state, but then it says nothing about area), and regions larger than half the 8 qubits.

### Stage 4d: the first law with a thermal reference (**Counted**; positive in a limited range)
Stage 4c used the ground state, where `K_A` is not `beta H_A`. With a Gibbs reference `sigma = e^{-beta H}/Z` (flow time 0.05, `thermal_first_law`) and the same local unitary excitation: (1) for the whole system `S_rel = beta Delta<H>` exactly (ratio 1.000000, since `Delta S = 0`; checked at `beta` 0.2, 1 and 5), which is Nariai's `S_rel = beta Delta E` and contains the Coulomb energy of the area dipoles `sum K_pq Delta<Z_p Z_q>` (`K = kappa W`, `kappa` fixed by the area per bit squared) plus the tunnelling energy; (2) for a three-pair region, `K_A = beta_eff H_A + const` holds with `beta_eff = beta` and residual 0.8% at `beta` 0.05, 1.6% at 0.1, 3% at 0.2, 7.5% at 0.5, but 13% at 1, 19% at 2, 30% at 5 and 35% at 20 (where `beta_eff` saturates at 3.7), and the regional relation `S_rel(rho_A || sigma_A) = beta Delta<H_A>` holds to 1% at `beta` 0.05, 2% at 0.2, 6% at 0.5, 10% at 1, then reaches 0.83 at 5 and 0.22 at 20 (6 pairs; 8 pairs and a second region agree, 1.19 at `beta` 1 for the less central region). So the Nariai-type local first law holds in the model in the hot regime (`beta` below about 0.5 in units where `J` = 0.3 and the strongest `K` is 0.8), i.e. when correlations are short and the region's modular Hamiltonian is local, and fails as the state approaches the entangled ground state where Stage 4 measured area-like entropy. It is not a statement that the area law holds there; no area change enters, only the energy `Delta<H_A>`. Not done: connecting `Delta<H_A>` to an area change through the Coulomb term, which would need `kappa` derived from the geometry (it is a definition of `toymodel_v6`).

### Stage 4e: the modular Hamiltonian in the cold regime (**Counted**; partly positive)
Where Stage 4d's local first law fails (the ground state), what is `K_A = -ln rho_A`? `pauli_coefficients` expands it in Pauli strings (the expansion reconstructs a random Hermitian matrix to 1e-12, and for a hot Gibbs state of the region the coefficient of `X_i` over `-J` equals `beta` to 1e-9, which checks the estimator). Ground state, flow time 0.05: (1) `K_A` is mostly one- and two-body and of the same kinds as `H_A`: for four of 8 pairs, `X` carries 37% and `ZZ` 53% of its squared norm (no `Z` or `Y` at all, `XX` 0.4%); for five of 12 pairs 20% and 74%; but for five of a different 10-pair cluster only 54% is one- or two-body (46% in three- to five-body strings), so locality is not generic. (2) The weight of `X_i` is a local inverse temperature `beta_loc = c_X / (-J)`, and it grows with the depth of the pair inside the region (distance from its position to the nearest pair outside): Spearman 0.63, 0.74 and 0.80 for regions of 3, 4 and 5 of 12 pairs (30, 40, 60 points; shuffled-depth null sd 0.08), mean `beta_loc` 3.3 to 4.2, scatter about 2. This is the Bisognano-Wichmann picture of a weight that rises from the boundary. (3) It does not survive to half the system: for regions of 6 of 12 pairs the correlation is 0.10 and the mean `beta_loc` is -0.28 (the `X` coefficient changes sign). **Limits:** one cluster, one flow time, 190 points, depth measured in tile-position units, no linearity in depth established (a pooled linear fit has slope 0.23 per unit and rms residual 2.7), and `ZZ` weights were not profiled. So the ground state's modular Hamiltonian has a depth-graded local temperature for regions up to about 40% of the system; that is a Rindler-like structure with boundary effects but not a demonstration of an area law.

### Stage 4f: depth profiles with `ZZ` weights, three depth measures, and 16 pairs (**Counted**; positive below half the system, reversed above)
Same ground-state modular Hamiltonian, 12 and 16 central pairs of generation 3 (`weight_profiles`, regions = compact regions of each size; single coefficients are read with `xz_coefficient`, checked against the full Pauli expansion to 1e-12). Local weights: `beta_loc = c_X / (-J)` and, for Delaunay-linked pairs with `|K|` above a quarter of the largest, `beta_ZZ = c_ZZ / K_pq`. **(1) The ZZ weight is the same kind of temperature as the X weight.** Mean `beta_ZZ` is 4.5, 4.3, 4.2 for regions of 3, 4, 5 of 12 pairs and 4.5, 4.5, 4.2 of 16 pairs, against mean `beta_loc` 3.3, 3.6, 4.2 and 2.9, 3.6, 3.7: one inverse temperature of about 4 (in units of this Hamiltonian) governs both terms, with scatter 0.9 to 1.3 for ZZ and 1.5 to 2.0 for X. This is the first test of the dipole `ZZ` coupling as a local energy weight and it passes for regions below about 40% of the system. **(2) Depth profile.** Spearman correlation of `beta_loc` with Euclidean depth: 0.63, 0.74, 0.80 (12 pairs, regions of 3, 4, 5) and 0.56, 0.79, 0.74 (16 pairs); of `beta_ZZ`: 0.50 and 0.57 (12 pairs, m = 4, 5), 0.43 and 0.38 (16 pairs), about 0 for m = 3 (13 to 18 points, sd of a shuffled null about 0.15 at these sample sizes). **(3) Which depth.** The Euclidean distance and the ledger depth (tile area of the region's pairs no deeper than the qubit, `2 A(1, b)` per pair) give nearly the same X correlation (0.67 to 0.74 for the ledger, m = 4, 5, both sizes); the ledger is weaker for ZZ at 16 pairs (0.24, 0.31 against 0.43, 0.38). The Delaunay hop count is clearly worse (0.08 to 0.38 for X, 0.05 to 0.36 for ZZ; it takes only 2 to 3 values): the profile follows the metric distance and the area enclosed, not the graph distance. The ledger depth is a monotone radial rank, so it cannot be a better test than the distance; what it shows is that area and distance agree here. **(4) Half the system: genuine, tied to `m / n`, not a finite-size artefact of one size.** Mean `beta_loc` against region size m: 12 pairs, 3.3, 3.6, 4.2, -0.3, -2.1 for m = 3 to 7; 16 pairs, 2.9, 3.6, 3.7, 2.1, -2.8, -3.6, -2.2, -0.8 for m = 3 to 10. The sign changes between m = 5 and 6 of 12 and between m = 6 and 7 of 16, i.e. at a fixed fraction (0.42 to 0.50 and 0.38 to 0.44 of the system), not at a fixed m; at m = 6 of 16 the mean is still positive (2.1) but the depth correlation has gone (0.16). Above half, the profile reverses: Spearman -0.82 (m = 7 of 12), -0.77, -0.78, -0.79 (m = 8, 9, 10 of 16) for X and -0.55 to -0.79 for ZZ, with negative mean `beta`. K_A of a region larger than half the system has the spectrum of K of its smaller complement, so a weight read off `H_A` terms is not the natural variable there. **Limits:** one flow time, one cluster at each size, compact regions only (the 16-pair set has 40 to 100 points per size), a quarter-of-max threshold for the ZZ pairs, and no larger system: whether the crossover fraction is constant as n grows is the open question (two sizes only).

### Stage 5: renormalisation and the semiclassical limit
Make the three tiers a coarse-graining ladder rather than three unrelated computers: a map from level `k` to `k-1` that preserves the Hamiltonian's low-energy sector, using the inflation `lambda^2` and the closed 53-type pair rule. Examine the spectral dimension, isotropy of the long-wave limit and how the dipole frustration (0.16 to 0.21 for up to 28 pairs; bulk value near 0.29 is a hypothesis) flows with scale.
Pass: a fixed point or a clean scaling of the couplings with level. Fail: couplings that do not close under the map.

### Stage 6: gravitational signatures, or a decision to stop
The earlier models found no monopole source, no horizon and the wrong sign for light bending. Either add an ingredient that can supply a source (a field on the lift lattice, a four-dimensional complex whose moves are Pachner moves, or a rule that gives the bit an energy), or state that the substrate is an error-correcting code and a toy Hamiltonian rather than a gravity model.
Pass: a source that gives a `1/r` potential or a Gauss-law flux with the right sign, tested on a patch. Otherwise record the negative.

### Stage 4g — negative temperature (β < 0) and the thermal first law

**Counted** (`relative_entropy.thermal_first_law`, region (0,1,2) of 6 pairs, flow 0.05; test `test_local_first_law_is_controlled_by_abs_beta_not_by_the_sign_of_the_temperature`):
- The global first law S_rel = βΔ⟨H⟩ holds exactly for either sign of β (ratio 1.0000 for β from −5 to 5).
- The regional law K_A ≈ β_eff·H_A has the same locality residual for +β and −β (0.008, 0.032, 0.084 at |β| = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5; 0.370 and 0.346 at |β| = 20) and a regional ratio that deviates from 1 with opposite sign.
- So the law is controlled by |β|, not by the sign of the temperature: it holds when the state is *hot* (small |β|), whether that heat is positive or negative.

**Not found**: negative temperature does not rescue the first law at large |β|. At β = −5 the regional ratio is 0.59 and the residual 0.33. The "first law holds only when hot" idea is supported; "negative kelvin" adds nothing here, because H is bounded, so −H is a mirror model.

**Side check, null**: the Berry curvature of the two-pair ground state over (J, chiral coupling) is zero to 1e-15, as it must be for a real Hamiltonian. Any Hopf-type phase fibre over parameter space would need a complex Hamiltonian, which this model does not have.

### Stage 4h — S⁷→S⁴ on pairs of pair-qubits, and tier-to-tier entanglement without bridges (`hopf_pairs.py`, 7 tests)

**Exact.** Write a two-qubit state (a, b, c, d) as q1 = a + bj, q2 = c + dj. The quaternionic Hopf map of the HopfFibration fork, (2 q1 q̄2, |q1|²−|q2|²), then has (x1, x2, x5) equal to the Bloch vector of qubit 1, and (x3, x4) of length C = 2|ad−bc|, the concurrence. So |r₁|² + C² = 1. The fibre (right multiplication) is a local SU(2) on qubit 2 and does not move the base point. S⁴ is "qubit 1 plus how entangled it is". Concurrence is *not* a function of x5 alone (a search for C = √(1−x5²) fails).

**Counted** (central pairs of generation 3, flow ground state). For a mixed pair of pair-qubits the map is undefined, but the leak D = (1−|r_p|²) − C² ≥ 0 measures how far the pair is from the pure two-qubit picture (D = 0 exactly when it is pure).
- 2 pairs: D = 0, so the S⁷→S⁴ picture is exact.
- 8 pairs, strongest-coupled partners, mean D: about 0.68 at flow 0.05 and about 0.06 at flow 0.2.
- 12 pairs: D 0.82 at flow 0.05, 0.11 at flow 0.2.
- So the picture applies only on the hot side of the flow, where the entanglement is mostly pairwise. In the cold cat regime nearly all of it is shared with the rest.

**Counted, negative / model-dependent.** The exact patches of different generations do not nest at one scale, so there is no natural common frame for cross-tier dipole couplings. Using the same dipole law between overlaid tiers (4 pairs per tier, generations 2, 3, 4):
- The ground state at flow ≤ 0.13 is the parity cat, and every tier pair has mutual information ln 2 = 0.693. That is GHZ-type parity correlation, not tier entanglement. With the doublet resolved into one classical pattern, tier mutual information is ≤ 0.0013 nats at flow 0.05.
- Real tier entanglement appears at flow 0.3 and 0.6. With the centred overlay it is 0.94, 0.56, 0.49 nats for (1,2), (2,3), (1,3) at flow 0.3. With the inflation-scaled overlay it is 0.05, 0.54, 0.03. The "nearest tier is most entangled" ordering therefore depends on the overlay choice and is not robust.
- Exact quench from |0…0⟩ with no bridges: tier mutual information 0.003–0.03 nats up to t = 16.
- The hand-chosen CX/CZ bridges give 0.21, 0.42, 0.26 nats after 3 cycles. Without bridges the network state is an exact product (0.000).
- Random offsets of the tiers at SD 3 and 10 give entanglement in every sample, so it does not need the Spectre geometry; the overlay (dipoles landing on top of each other, cross couplings up to 55× the intra-tier ones) drives it.

**Verdict.** Bridge-free tier entanglement exists only as a consequence of how the tiers are overlaid; the model does not currently derive that overlay. Deriving the tier-to-tier coupling from the substitution rule (a tile of tier k+1 containing its tier-k children) is the open item.
