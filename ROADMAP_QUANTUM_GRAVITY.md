# Roadmap: from a Spectre qubit network toward a quantum-gravity theory

This is a plan, not a result. Nothing in this repository or in its companion papers is a theory of quantum gravity, and the stages below are ordered by what can be tested next, not by how close the goal is. Each stage has a deliverable and a pass/fail test, so that a stage can be abandoned honestly.

Companion repositories: `spectre-monotile-py` (exact tilings, flips, pair Hamiltonian; paper `quantgravedit.tex`, long draft `quantgrav.tex`), `wada-coudene` (window and flip engine), `spectre` (Hat and Spectre source, `SPECTRE_DIR`).

Labels used throughout: **Exact** (proved or finite exact computation), **Counted/Measured** (numerical, one setting), **Negative** (a natural expectation refuted), **Hypothesis** (untested).

## 1. What a theory has to supply

A discrete approach to quantum gravity needs, at minimum, the following. The right-hand columns say where the Spectre programme stands.

| # | Requirement | Status in this programme | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | A background-independent, local move set | Phason flips are clusters of about ten tiles at uniform density, not single moves; whether clusters are valid tilings, and whether they are Pachner-type moves, is undecided | Measured; open |
| R2 | A Hilbert space and a Hamiltonian | One qubit per bump-and-dent pair, `H_eff = -J sum tau^x - sum K_AB tau^z tau^z`, dipolar frustrated couplings; the unique classical ground state is known exactly to 28 pairs | Exact |
| R3 | Geometry that is dynamical, not fixed | The contact graph changes in 75% of tile-changing flips; the pair count changes while the area ledger is kept. No rule yet turns this into a unitary or a quantum channel | Measured; open |
| R4 | A semiclassical limit (continuum, isotropy, diffeomorphism-like symmetry) | The inflation `lambda^2 = 4 + sqrt(15)` gives a scale ladder and the pair-rule table closes at 53 types from level 4; no continuum limit of the Hamiltonian is defined | Exact for the rule; open for the limit |
| R5 | Sources and a Newtonian limit | None: the tile as it stands has no monopole source, no horizon and no light bending of the right sign | Negative |
| R6 | An arrow or an ordering of time | None from flips (the relation is symmetric); Hamiltonians are real so entropy growth is time-symmetric about the initial state | Negative |
| R7 | Observables that look gravitational: area laws, entanglement geometry, error correction | Erasure is unambiguous (hole of 300 tiles has one refilling), which is the local-recoverability half of the Li-Boyle code condition; no entropy or area-law measurement on the network yet | Exact for refilling; open |
| R8 | Matter and gauge fields | A Z2 gauge form on the patch complex and an exact flux law exist; the lifted tetrad flux is zero | Exact; no dynamics |

The programme is strongest at R2 and R7 (substrate, Hamiltonian, recoverability) and weakest at R4 to R6. The roadmap therefore starts by making the simulator match the exact structure, and only then asks the gravitational questions.

## 2. Stages

### Stage 0: make this repository say what it does (done)
Problems found by running the first version, and what was done (all checked by `--tests-only`):

- *Tile counts.* The tiers had 8, 63 and 496 tiles instead of 9, 71, 559, because a Mystic (two tiles) was counted as one object. The exact builder gives 9, 71, 559, 4401 for singles and 8, 62, 488, 3842 for Mystics.
- *Substitution.* Children were placed by sector offsets, so the patches were not tilings. The patches are now the exact edge-matched tilings of `spectre_exact.py` (integer `Z[zeta_12]`), tested for the tile shape and area, for no overlap, and for edge matching.
- *Qubits.* Qubits now sit on bump-and-dent pairs (6, 82, 804 at generations 2, 3, 4), not on 4 hub vertices. The 9-tile patch has no pair, so the tiers are generations 2, 3, 4. The number of qubits per tier is still a budget (the central pairs), because the joint state is dense.
- *Feedback.* It was inert: a Hadamard on every qubit gives `<Z> = 0` for all later RZ and CZ gates, and `curve_strength` cannot change the area of a point-symmetric Bezier tile. The state now starts in `|0..0>`, evolves under the pair Hamiltonian (Trotter steps tested against exact diagonalisation), and `<Z_p>` moves the flat vertex, changing the two areas by `+-a h <Z_p>` with the pair area conserved.
- *Joint state.* The old code projected the joint state onto invented marginal amplitudes; one joint `State` is now kept and read directly.
- *Docs.* The README module table named files that do not exist; it now matches the files.

Not done: `qubits_per_tier` is small (4 by default), so most pairs carry no qubit; the tiers differ only in how many pairs exist, and the three tiers' central pairs are locally alike, so they give similar `<Z>`.

### Stage 1: an exact substrate (mostly done)
Done: exact edge-matched tilings, qubits on pairs, the area ledger in the geometry, and pair counts 6, 82, 804, 7012 reproduced for generations 2 to 4 (the generation 5 count and the 53-type pair-rule table are not yet recovered inside this repository; they are in `spectre-monotile-py`).
Pass: a test recovering the pair-rule table from the patch, and pair counts to generation 5.

### Stage 2: dynamics of the qubits (done for 6 to 16 pairs; J and kappa from the flow, as definitions)
Load the dipolar pair Hamiltonian into the QWLA `State` simulator for small clusters (6 to 14 pairs, matrix-free) and compare with the exact diagonalisation in `research/toymodel_v6` and `v10`. Measure the ground-state concurrence, CHSH of reduced pairs, and the gap along the Spectre-to-Hat parameter.
Finding so far (`figures/fig_dynamics.png`): with the geometric dipole couplings (`|W| <= 0.06` in units of `(a h)^2` for the central pairs) and the former defaults `J = 1`, `kappa = 0.09`, the couplings were about 1% of the tunnelling and the pairs nearly independent; the Trotter evolution agrees with exact diagonalisation to 1e-4 at `J = 0.05`, `kappa = 1` and to 1.4e-4 at the flow parameters below. The network now defaults to the Spectre-to-Hat flow (below), where the frustration matters.
Pass: agreement with the exact values (cat ground state at zero tunnelling; largest pairwise concurrence at an intermediate `tau`). A mismatch is a bug in one of the two programs.

**Done in this repository (`pair_hamiltonian.py`, `figures/fig_stage2_tau_scan.png`; **Exact** or **Counted**, central pairs of generation 3, 6 to 16 pairs):**
- The matrix-free `State` evolution agrees with exact evolution; second-order (Strang) steps converge as `dt^2` in the state vector, first-order as `dt`. The agreement is checked at `kappa = 20`, where `kappa W` is near 1; at the default scale the two terms nearly commute and the check is trivial.
- Zero tunnelling: the unique classical ground state is a doublet (`s`, `-s`), the ground state is the even cat state, its entropy across any cut is exactly 1 bit, and the cat-doublet splitting falls from 1.6e-4 (6 pairs) to 2e-13 (16 pairs) at `tau = 0.1`. The parity-sector solver separates this doublet exactly; a plain Lanczos solver returns an arbitrary mix of the two states, which would make every entanglement measure depend on solver noise.
- Intermediate `tau`: the largest pairwise concurrence peaks at `tau` of 0.4 to 1.2 for every size, **but it does not grow with size** (0.38, 0.52, 0.65, 0.51, 0.44, 0.35 at 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16 pairs) and the CHSH value above 2 (up to 2.33 at 10 pairs) is gone at 16. The reading is a finite-size effect plus monogamy as the cluster grows. It is **not** evidence of a phase or of a persistent Bell-violating network. The pair of 16 comes from generation 4, the others from generation 3, so the sizes are not a pure scan.
- The gap along the Spectre-to-Hat parameter is **not done** (exact patches need `a = b = 1`).

**`J`, `kappa` and `h` from the flow (done as definitions; a derivation is still open).** The scan shows that the model changes character at `tau = J / (kappa w)` of about 0.3 to 1.2, while the former default `J = 1`, `kappa = 0.09` was `tau` of 190 to 260 across the three tiers (a product state). `toymodel_v6` (spectre-monotile-py) defines a flow from the Spectre to the Hat: `h(tau) = tan(60 degrees (1 - tau))`, tunnelling `J = exp(-h^2 / 2)` and coupling `kappa = kappa_norm h^2`, with `kappa_norm` fixing the strongest coupling of generation 2 at the Spectre end to 1. `pair_hamiltonian.flow_parameters` implements it and `TriSpectreNetwork` now defaults to it at the Spectre end (and for `flow_tau > 0` the tiles and couplings are recomputed at `b(tau)` from the exact vertices, `x_b = (p + q w) + b z (r + s w)`, which matches `toymodel_v6`'s independent patch code to 1e-14 in the couplings and to 6 printed digits in concurrence, CHSH and gap at seven flow times; the rescaled `flow_scan` is kept as a labelled approximation, off by at most 0.014 in concurrence) (`h = sqrt(3)`, `J = 0.223`, `kappa = 17.4`, `tau` of 0.22 to 0.30 per tier). Checks: `kappa_norm`, `tau` of 0.244 and the largest concurrence 0.407 of 10 central pairs reproduce `toymodel_v6` (independent patch code); along the flow `tau` rises from 0.24 to infinity, so the flow crosses the window, and the concurrence peaks at flow time 0.05 (`tau` of 0.42). **Limits:** the functional forms of `h(tau)`, the overlap and `kappa` are `toymodel_v6`'s Definitions, so this fixes `J / kappa` by a stated rule, not by a derivation; and `h = sqrt(3)` is safe for `curve_strength` 0.5 but crosses at 0.8.

### Stage 3: flips as dynamics
A flip changes which tiles touch, so the Hilbert space (the set of pairs) changes. Define one rule for how a flip acts on the qubits: pairs that persist keep their state, pairs that are created or destroyed are initialised or traced out, and the area ledger and the pair-parity flux are conserved. Then test conservation, reversibility (the flip relation is symmetric, so the rule should be) and whether the resulting channel is unitary on a fixed extended space.
Prerequisite from `spectre-monotile-py`: decide whether flip clusters are tilings (exact refilling of the holes of valid flips).
Pass: a flip channel that conserves the ledger and is its own inverse in the reversed step; fail: any rule that needs the pair count to be conserved (it is not).

**Stage 3, first measurement (`flip_rule.py` 9 checks, `flip_run.py` 3 checks, `flip_pairs.py`, `patch_flips.py`; class 0, level 3, `delta` 0.03, validated radius 8.5; created pairs start in `|+>`).** Written 2026-11-25; the data are the region-level flips of the wada-coudene engine, not closed clusters (those need radius about 26).

*Set-up.* The wada-coudene engine (`PBOX` 20, `R2` 14, `DIAM` 5.5, `IN_T/OUT_T` 0.0015/0.0035, 3000 phases) gives 2242 unambiguous phases, 678 with a change of the tile set, 605 of them valid tilings of the region before and after (89%, as in `SPECTRE_FLIPS.md`). `flip_rule.find_pairs_keys` applies the pair rule of `find_pairs` to the exact vertex keys of the placed tiles: it gives 6, 82 and 804 on the generation 2, 3 and 4 patches (**Exact**) and 9.4 pairs in the region on average (all of them two single-window tiles; no pair contains a Mystic leaf, 6028 of 6028). Couplings are the dipole ones at `b(tau)`; the roles of the two tiles of a pair are fixed by the order of their centroids, which is a conjugation by `X_p` and leaves the spectrum unchanged (checked against `pair_couplings`: equal `|W|`, equal spectra).

*What a flip does to the pairs (**Counted**, 605 flips).* 383 leave the pair set unchanged although tiles change, 222 change it, and 111 change the pair count, by -4 to +4 (494 have the same count). The pair count is therefore not conserved, and no linear law in the tile classes holds: against the changes of single-window and Mystic leaves the fit has R^2 0.29, and of the 427 flips that keep the leaf count (the area ledger inside the region) 44 still change the pair count. The 40 control phases with no tile change have no pair change. Every changed tile is one of the same orientation class here (a reflected transform), so an orientation-parity count is not informative in this window class.

*The three rules (**Exact** as maps, **Counted** on the 222 events at flow time 0.05, `J` 0.306, `kappa` 13.7).* Ground state of the old region, new pairs in `|+>`.
- *Dormant* (the qubit of a destroyed pair is kept, the slot is switched off; the rule is a permutation of the presence flags on register x presence). Unitary (`U^dagger U = 1`), its own inverse, flip then reversed flip returns the state with overlap 1. **Pass**, and it does not need the pair count conserved.
- *Reset* (destroyed qubit replaced by `|+>`, Kraus `|+><m|`) and *trace* (destroyed qubits traced out, created ones appended in `|+>`). Trace-preserving, not unitary; they agree on the present register to 1e-12. Flip then reversed flip returns the old state with overlap 0.32 +- 0.02 (204 of 222 events are irreversible; the 18 that only create pairs are exact). **Fail** the reversibility criterion.
- All three give the same energy and the same present-register state, so what separates them is the information: destroyed pairs carry 0.96 +- 0.02 bit of entanglement with the persisting ones (0.44 bit per destroyed pair), which dormant keeps and the other two discard.
- Energy after the flip, in the old state under `H(pi1)`: `dE` = 1.87 +- 0.10, above the new ground energy by 1.82 +- 0.08; the overlap of the persisting marginal with the new ground state is 0.20 +- 0.02 (above 0.5 in 18% of events). With `|+>` a created pair adds exactly `-J` and no coupling energy (`<Z> = 0`); `|0>` would add `+kappa` times the sum of `W` over the created pairs instead (not run).
- Shuffled null (same numbers of destroyed pairs, drawn at random from the old region): the entanglement lost per flip (0.972 bit, Wilcoxon p = 0.13) and the return overlap (0.310, p = 0.44) are the same, so flips do not choose entangled pairs; `dE` is higher for flips (1.87 against 1.63, p = 5e-5), so they destroy pairs with slightly more bond energy. The null for the overlap with the new ground state is not meaningful (created tiles land on kept ones) and is not used.
- The pure-state formulas used for 13 pairs were checked against the density-matrix rules on 12 real events and 5 synthetic ones, deviation 1.5e-14.

*Towards the Pachner classification (**Counted** for pair moves; **Negative** for tile moves).* Tile moves: the engine finds no closed cluster inside radius 8.5 (0 of the clusters, as in `SPECTRE_FLIPS.md`), so `(dV, dE, dF)` and the Pachner type of a flip are not obtained here. Pair moves: the destroyed and created pairs of one flip, linked when their midpoints are within 3.5 to 5.0 (the result is the same over that range), form local moves of at most two pairs on each side: 1 to 1 (164), 2 to 2 (144), 1 to 0 (63 to 65), 0 to 1 (45 to 67), 2 to 1 (24), 1 to 2 (20), 0 to 2 (11). 65% conserve the pair number (a pair hops by about a tile width or two pairs rearrange); the 1 to 0, 0 to 1, 2 to 1 and 1 to 2 moves change it. The events with three or four pairs destroyed and created (50 of type (3,3), 38 of type (4,4)) split into these moves at those thresholds.

*Towards the 82- and 804-pair states.* The pair rule and the dormant rule are size independent, and `patch_flips.py` builds the generation 3 patch from the engine's own window points (496 window tiles, 559 leaves, every tile inside its window, 82 pairs, no overlapping neighbour missing from the placement lists in a sample of 60 tiles). **Negative** for flips of that patch: it sits at a non-generic phase. Over 24 phason directions, at `delta` 0.004 no tile leaves its window in any; at 0.01 none does in 22 directions and in the other two all 496 tiles sit in the ambiguous band at once; at 0.03 none does in 22 and up to 30 do in the other two. A generic phase changes about 10 tiles per phase within radius 14 (`SPECTRE_FLIPS.md`), so about 75 would be expected in this patch. A phason step of the substitution patch is therefore not a set of local flips. Flips of a generic patch of that size would need the engine at a radius of about 40 (an estimate: about 75 000 placements) and a faster validity test than its pairwise overlap check. No 82- or 804-pair state exists in this repository to apply a rule to: the QMC gives thermal `Z` correlators only and is not converged at those sizes (Stage 4), so neither the entanglement lost nor the overlap with the new ground state can be stated there; for the energy it would give `dE = J sum_D <X> - kappa sum <W Z Z> - J |C|` from correlators that Stage 4 says are not converged.

*Closed clusters from a generic phase (`generic_flips.py`; **Negative**, 2026-11-26).* Moving the 559-tile patch by one large step (`delta` 0.2 to 0.4) gives a tiling `T(s)` (checked: 519 to 526 tiles that cover the patch exactly once, every window point inside its window). Small steps from `T(s)` are still not local flips: in 24 directions at `delta` 0.03 the number of removed tiles takes a few values only (0, 3, 33, 38, 73), the same over runs of 3 to 6 neighbouring directions, with more tiles added than removed (46 for 38, 86 for 73); at 0.1 up to 128 are removed. Over six phases and 24 directions the clusters were 2726 ambiguous (a tile at distance 0.0015 to 0.0035 from its window), 818 one-sided and 151 near the boundary, and none closed. The window points of a substitution patch are a self-similar set, so tiles leave in blocks; I did not check why 818 clusters have only removed or only added tiles (a missing replacement would be a hole, so this needs a look before the numbers are trusted). So generic local flips need a phase that is not derived from a substitution patch: the engine at radius about 40 with its own random phases, which is the large run and is not done.

*Closed clusters from a generic cut-and-project phase (`generic_phase.py`, `exact_window.py`; **Negative: the flips are open ribbons**, 2026-11-26).* Phases `y0` are random, not derived from a patch; the tiling of the disc is checked (covered area equals disc area to 1e-12, no ambiguous tile in the disc, 6 of 6 phases at R=36 and 3 of 3 at R=60). Window membership uses the exact graph-directed recursion (`exact_window.py`; agrees with the depth-7 cloud on 99.9% of 40,000 random points and leaves the ambiguous band about 1e-4 wide instead of 3.5e-3). With it the ambiguous clusters fall from 1737 to 3 and the interior one-sided cluster of the first pilot disappears (it came from the old band). Result: at R=36 (6 phases, 24 directions, `delta` 0.03) 993 clusters, none closed and none inside the region; at R=60 (3 phases, 12 directions, `delta` 0.03 and 0.015) 308 and 174 clusters, none closed. The clusters that are not edge tiles are large (26 to 272 tiles, diameters 26 to 156), the same cluster turns up in several directions and at both step sizes, and their removed and added footprints agree except for a symmetric difference that is a multiple of 8.2 in area and lies wholly within 8 of the region edge (checked for 12 distinct clusters at R=60, `symdiff_inner` = 0 for all). So each flip at a generic phase is a ribbon of tiles that crosses the region. *Ribbon geometry (`TAG=_rib` runs, R=60, 3 phases, 12 directions each; Counted).* (1) Ends: every end component is an added-tile fragment (area 3.0, 5.2 or 11.2, one tile) at radius 72 to 82, which is the edge of the enumerated candidates (`RP` = R+16 = 76), so the ends are enumeration artefacts and say nothing about where a ribbon stops; the ribbons reach at least radius 72 in both directions. The end tile classes are not one fixed pair (for example classes 8 and 8, 5 and 7, 9 and 10, 4 and 11), which is what the artefact predicts. (2) Length: the tile count of a ribbon is set by the chord it cuts from the disc (tiles per unit extent 0.95, sd 0.39, 30 ribbons at `delta` 0.06) and the same sizes recur at 0.015, 0.03 and 0.06 (3, 4, 5, 6, 21, 24, 31, 43, 45, 60, 63 tiles), so the length does not depend on the step in this range. The step changes the number of distinct ribbons per run: 2, 11, 16, 41 at `delta` 0.0075, 0.015, 0.03, 0.06, about linear above 0.015, and at 0.06 some clusters have 3 to 7 end components (ribbons that touch merge). The earlier statement that ribbons "close only outside the region" was too strong: the data say only that they do not close inside radius 72. Radius test (phase 1, `delta` 0.03, 12 directions, `generic_phase_d0.03_R100_rib.json`; Counted): at R=100 the end components moved out with the edge, to radius 112 to 122 (R+16 again), the longest ribbons grew from 156 to 247 in diameter (up to 362 tiles; at R=60 up to 135), and the symmetric difference inside the region is still 0 for all 11 ribbons. So no ribbon closes inside radius 100, and every end follows the enumeration edge, which is what an infinite ribbon predicts. Pairs of adjacent ribbons (Counted over the five `_rib` files, 81 distinct clusters): clusters that join several touching ribbons exist (more than two end components: 0 at `delta` 0.0075 and 0.015, 1 of 16 at 0.03 and 6 of 11 at R=100, 19 of 41 at 0.06), but none has zero end components, and all 245 end fragments are added-tile fragments, none removed, so no two ends can cancel each other. Each ribbon is already area-conserving everywhere in the interior; what stops it from being a closed cluster is that it runs to the edge, and pairing it with a neighbour does not change that. One phase and one step size only, so the radius test is evidence, not a proof; a proof would need an argument from the window geometry.
*Pair events along ribbons (`PAIRS=1 TAG=_pair`, R=60, 3 phases, 12 directions, `delta` 0.015, 0.03, 0.06; Counted).* For each ribbon with at least 20 tiles, the bump-and-dent pairs destroyed and created were counted inside the region at distance 8 or more from its edge (`len_in` is the extent of the removed tiles along the ribbon's principal axis, up to about 100). Of 40 ribbon samples, 15 are pair-silent (no pair event; 40 to 64 tiles, the same few sizes 43, 45, 60, 63 and 64 recurring) and 25 are active (70 to 215 tiles). For the active ribbons (total length 2084) 496 pairs are destroyed and 500 created, i.e. 0.238 and 0.240 per unit length, so pair number is conserved along a ribbon on average (net -2 to +6 per run, individual ribbons within +-2 of zero); per ribbon the event rate (destroyed plus created) is 0.46 per unit length (sd 0.14, range 0.10 to 0.98). Events come in sites about 3.3 apart (556 gaps, median 2.4, coefficient of variation 0.64) with 1.3 to 2.1 events per site; the gaps cluster near 1 to 2 and near 6 to 8, so there are two spacings, not one period and not a Poisson sequence (which would have coefficient 1). Caveats: the same ribbon is counted at more than one `delta` (they are not independent samples); the rate is per unit length of the ribbon inside radius 52; one length scale only, no error bars from independent phases. For Stage 3 this gives the pair-event rate for one ribbon flip as 0.24 destroyed and 0.24 created per unit length, which replaces the per-cluster counts of the patch results (those were local clusters of the substitution patch and do not occur at generic phases). *Why some ribbons are pair-silent (`TAG=_cls`, `delta` 0.03 and 0.06, 35 ribbon samples, about 25 distinct; Counted, no cause found).* The tile-class mix of silent and active ribbons is not clearly different (class fractions differ by at most 0.06; correlation of the event rate with a class fraction is at most 0.36 in size over 12 classes, not significant after the 12 comparisons), and silent ribbons use 8 to 10 of the 12 classes, as active ones do. What does separate them in the sample is size: the 11 silent samples have 40 to 64 tiles (diameters 36 to 128; 0.4 to 0.7 tiles per unit diameter for the three with diameter above 95) and the active ones 68 to 215 tiles at 0.6 to 1.0 tiles per unit diameter, except one active ribbon of 31 tiles near radius 57. So silence goes with sparse ribbons, but 35 samples with repeats do not settle that. *Geometry of the flat vertices (`TAG=_cat`, same runs, 35 samples; Counted).* For every leaf of a removed tile (in the old tiling) and of an added tile (in the new one) I counted how many tile vertices coincide at its flat vertex (index 10): alone, two with both flat (a pair), two with one not flat, or three or more. Silent ribbons: of 807 removed and 825 added leaves, none is a pair; 96% have three or more vertices meeting at the flat vertex, 2% a non-flat partner, 1% none. Active ribbons: of 3913 removed and 3996 added leaves, 19.8% and 20.6% are pair members, 77% and 76% have three or more vertices, 1.4% and 2.4% a non-flat partner. So a silent ribbon is silent because none of its tiles has a flat vertex at a pair site, in the old or the new tiling, not because the pair rule fails after the flip; the class mix and the tile count are not the cause (previous paragraph). The one active ribbon with 31 tiles has 14 pair leaves of 39 (36%), the most of any ribbon, which fits its high event rate for its size. *Environment test (`TAG=_env`, `delta` 0.03 and 0.06, 35 ribbon samples; Counted; the two-state idea is half right).* The pair-site fraction (leaves whose flat vertex is a pair, out of all leaves) of the whole tiling inside radius 52 is 0.447 to 0.458 for the six phases. Around the ribbons it is ordinary: for tiles within 4 of the ribbon (not removed ones) it is 0.548 for silent ribbons (range 0.531 to 0.567) and 0.479 for active ones (0.405 to 0.560), and for the shell 4 to 8 away 0.431 and 0.537. So a silent ribbon does not sit in a pair-free stretch of tiling. The difference is in the ribbon's own tiles: the pair fraction of removed leaves is exactly 0 for all 11 silent samples and 0.13 to 0.36 (mean 0.22) for the 24 active ones, with a gap between 0 and 0.13 and no values in between, i.e. two states for the ribbon, not for its surroundings. Ribbon tiles are also depleted in pair sites even when active (0.22 against 0.45 for the tiling). The event rate does not follow the pair fraction of the ribbon's tiles among active ribbons (correlation 0.07) or that of the environment (-0.16); the overall correlation of -0.65 with the near-band fraction comes from the silent group alone. Caveats: samples are repeated across `delta`; the near band has 148 leaves for silent and 293 for active ribbons (silent ribbons are shorter in the region). *What the silent ribbons share (`TAG=_tile`, 35 samples; Counted).* (1) Tile shape: the classes 5 and 7 to 11 are the two-leaf G tiles and the classes 0 to 4 and 6 the one-leaf O tiles; no removed G tile in any ribbon carries a pair-site leaf (0 of 1129), 774 of 2462 removed O tiles do. Silent ribbons contain O tiles of all six O classes (28 to 91 each) with no pair site, so the shape does not explain silence. (2) Window position: for a removed O tile the window point `z` in its own window decides the pair site. Pair sites have `z_y` between -1.1 and 0.3 (the lower edge of the O window), non-pair tiles `z_y` mostly above 0; the cut `z_y` < 0 classifies 91% of the 2462 correctly. The share of a ribbon's O tiles with `z_y` < 0 is 0.07 (median, range 0.05 to 0.11) for silent ribbons and 0.33 (0.26 to 0.52) for active ones. So a ribbon is silent when the tiles it moves have window points on the upper boundary of the O window, where no flat vertex is a pair site. (3) Tile-type order along a ribbon is not what separates them: both kinds show O and G tiles in a repeating motif (for example G O O repeated, with class runs such as 3 3 11 2 2 2 7 2 1 5 5 repeating in one silent ribbon), which I did not quantify. Figures: `figures/ribbon_1_silent_vs_active.png` (a silent and an active ribbon with their pair sites), `ribbon_2_window_position.png` (pair status against window position), `ribbon_3_statistics.png` (ribbon size against region, ends at the enumeration edge, gaps between pair sites); drawn by `make_ribbon_figures.py`. Not tested: how the silent or active character depends on the step direction (the same ribbon recurs over several directions, which suggests it does not, but I did not check), or whether the O-window rule is exact once the window boundary is resolved more finely than the 91% cut.
Not done: why the silent ribbons' flat vertices are all surrounded (a ribbon of one tile type sequence, or a position in the window), and whether the 0% and 20% are the two values of a two-state environment.

*Closed flips exist on the sphere: the Hat Trick ball (`hat_flips.py`, 4 tests, needs `brentharts/hexasphere`; **Exact**, 2026-11-26).* In the plane the flips are ribbons that do not close; a sphere is compact, so the question becomes finite. On the 480 kites of `GP(3,0)` (60 hats, 12 pentagonal holes) every connected cluster of up to 7 hats of the icosahedral tiling was removed and re-tiled in all ways. (1) The only flips are 12, one per pentagon: the ring of 5 hats around a pentagon (40 kites, an annulus, Euler characteristic 0) has exactly two tilings. (2) The 12 rings are disjoint and cover the ball, and an exact cover of all 480 kites gives 4096 = 2^12 tilings, exactly the 2^12 ring states; the two icosahedral tilings of the `hexasphere` README are all-0 and all-1, and its counts (2 for `I` and `T`, 16 for `C5`) are the invariant bit strings. (3) The number of vertices of the hat complex is `V = 80 - (number of icosahedron edges whose two rings are both flipped)` for all 4096 states, with `dF = 0` and `dE = dV`: an exact nearest-neighbour function on the icosahedron, and the Pachner-type data that Stage 3 could not get in the plane. So here a flip is a closed ribbon around a cone point (the 60 degree deficits of Stage 7), the configuration space is one bit per cone point, and the flip rule is `X` on that bit: unitary, no slot created or destroyed. Limits: this is the hat on one sphere, fixed hand, with the kite grid fixed; it is not a statement about the Spectre, and Stage 10a found no hat tilings on the other `GP(m, n)` tested. **Not done:** the relation of these 12 bits to the 12-puncture space of Stage 9b (dimension and `T` irreps there), whether a closed Spectre fold of Stage 7 has ring flips around its cone points, and a Hamiltonian (`V` as the energy gives a classical antiferromagnet-like `n_i n_j` term with no transverse field).

*The 12 ring bits and the 12-puncture space of Stage 9b (`ring_bits.py`, 4 tests; **Exact** for the identities; the reading of a bit as a spin is a definition).* (1) The 60 rotations act on the 4096 tilings by permuting the 12 bits, with no bit flipped (checked on 360 random tilings), so the action on `(C^2)^12` is the permutation action of Stage 9b; there are 96 tilings up to rotation. (2) Read a ring bit as a spin-1/2 at its cone point. Then the 132-dimensional singlet space of Stage 9b is a subspace of the span of the tilings: it is supported on the 924 tilings with exactly 6 rings flipped; 10 singlets are invariant under the 60 rotations (20 under `T`, as in Stage 9b). So where the pair qubits failed (a gauge-invariant pair is one classical bit), the ring bits fit as the punctures themselves, one spin per cone point, and the price is the same as in Stage 9b: a single bit is not gauge invariant, only the 132 combinations are. (3) The vertex count `V = 80 - sum_<ij> n_i n_j` does not commute with total spin, and its projection onto the singlets is exactly `P V P = 72.5 - (1/3) sum_<ij> S_i.S_j` (error 2e-13): the Heisenberg model on the icosahedron. On the singlets `sum S_i.S_j` runs from -6.1879 (unique, next level -5.6544) to 3.5275 (unique), so `P V P` runs from 71.32 to 74.56, against 50 to 80 for the classical tilings. So a quantum Hamiltonian appears with no transverse field put in by hand: it comes from the geometric count plus gauge invariance. Which sign of `V` is the energy is a choice that nothing here fixes. (4) On all 4096 states, `H = kappa sum n_i n_j - J sum X_i` (the flip as `X`; the form of `pair_hamiltonian.py`, and of a Rydberg blockade model on the icosahedron): at `J = 0` the ground space is the 69 tilings with no two neighbouring rings flipped; any `J > 0` selects one state (gap 0.18, 0.57, 1.92 at `J/kappa` = 0.1, 0.3, 1). With the other sign the all-flipped tiling is the ground state with gap 5. `J` and `kappa` are free here; no flow fixes them as in Stage 2. **Not done:** flat connections at level `k`, a derivation of the spin reading, dynamics.

*Closed Spectre folds (`spectre_fold_flips.py`, needs `brentharts/ballgen`; **Counted**, lower bounds).* Clusters of 1 to 4 tiles on 76 closed folds (patches of 4 to 21 tiles, seed 5) and of 1 to 5 tiles in the level-3 patch were re-tiled by an exact combinatorial search on the boundary angle word (straight-edged Spectre, one hand). Only clusters with at most one cone point inside were counted, because then the flat disc is fixed by its boundary and a second tiling is a flip of the same surface. Result: with one cone inside, 151 of 1079 clusters of 2 to 4 tiles have two or more tilings (47 of 301 two-tile clusters, 59 of 389 three-tile, 45 of 389 four-tile; up to 4 tilings); one two-tile case was confirmed by an independent enumeration of all gluings of two tiles. With no cone inside, no second tiling was found: 0 of 1243 clusters on the folds and 0 of 12,664 in the plane. The cone points of these folds are mostly corners of one tile zipped onto itself, so the flip is not a ring of 5 tiles as for the hat but a cone tile and one to three neighbours. The search misses tilings in which a tile splits the region (it does not find the original tiling for 3351 of the planar clusters), so "none found" is weak and only the positive counts are results. Together with the hat: on both closed surfaces the closed flips found sit at cone points, and none was found in flat regions, in agreement with the ribbons of the plane. **Not done:** drawing a flip, clusters with two or more cones (these mix flips with re-foldings, Stage 8), the pair qubits under such a flip.

*Verdict.* Pass for the dormant rule (unitary, reversible, no conserved pair count needed); fail for reset and trace on reversibility. Not done: closed clusters and the Pachner type, flips of a generic 82- or 804-pair region, a quantum state at those sizes, other window classes, level 4, `delta` other than 0.03, `|0>` as created state, and a test that separates the rules by the pair-parity flux.

*Pair-parity flux (**Exact**, from `quantgravalt.tex`, Result `flux-transfer`).* The flux through a set `S` of plaquettes equals the product of `Z_l` over the cut of `S`; the pair parity `-Z_{A1} Z_{A2}` (the Wilson-line product at `P8`) commutes with every term of the tile Hamiltonian and is conserved along the flow. It equals +1 in the ledger sector `Q = 0`. All three rules keep every pair qubit in that sector (a created pair in `|+>` is in it), so the flux is conserved by dormant, reset and trace alike and does not decide between them; it could do so only on the unreduced space of two tile bits per pair, which was not simulated. This replaces the earlier plan to use orientation-parity counts, which are uninformative here (every changed tile has the same orientation class). The dormant rule is the planar counterpart of the flip channel of `quantgravalt.tex` Result `sphere-channel` (Eq. `hjoint`: a slot keeps its state while switched off).

### Stage 4: geometry from entanglement (first measurement; inconclusive; QMC attempted at 82 and 804 pairs)

**QMC beyond exact diagonalisation (`qmc_pairs.py`, `qmc_stage4.py`; **Counted** for the validation, negative for the large sizes).** The Hamiltonian is stoquastic, so a path integral has no sign problem: discrete Trotter slices (`dtau = 0.1`), single-spin, segment and whole-world-line flips, and parallel tempering in `beta` (replicas with smaller `dtau` at fixed slice number, swaps exact). *Validated* against exact diagonalisation (thermal, flow time 0.05, `beta` 25 to 40): 3, 6, 8 pairs agree within noise, and the tempered sampler gives energy within 0.015 and `<Z_i Z_j>` within 0.02 to 0.05 (maximum over pairs) at 10, 12 and 16 pairs after 80 000 sweeps (a plain chain at 10 pairs needs 100 000 for 0.08; without tempering shorter runs are stuck in different classical basins, errors up to 0.9). So Stage 4 at 82 and 804 pairs is *not* answered; the 12 to 20 pair exact results above remain the evidence.

**First measurement (`entanglement_geometry.py`, `figures/fig_stage4_geometry.png`; **Counted**, exact ground states of 12 to 20 central pairs of generation 4). Inconclusive, not closed, not passed:**

### Stage 4b: relative entropy, from `NariaiRelativeEntropy` (**Counted**; negative for a direct transplant)
The sibling fork `brentharts/NariaiRelativeEntropy` derives the area law from the Araki-Umegaki relative entropy `S(rho || sigma)` of a perturbed state against a reference (`S_rel = 2 pi dQ`, the first law of the modular Hamiltonian, on the compact horizon of `dS2 x S2`). Three things carry over and are implemented in `relative_entropy.py`: (i) the relative entropy of regions of the flow ground state, `S(rho_A(tau + delta) || rho_A(tau))`, computed from eigendecompositions with the fork's two numerical traps handled (a state with weight where the reference has none is `inf`, never a clipped log; eigenvalues below the noise floor are dropped before `x ln x`); (ii) the first law `S_rel = Delta<K> - Delta S` with `K = -ln sigma`, checked to 1e-10 on random mixed states together with positivity and Pinsker; (iii) monotonicity under restriction, `S_A <= S_B` for `A` in `B`, checked on random pure states and on the flow ground states.
**Results (flow time 0.05, `delta` = 0.004, 8 to 14 central pairs of generation 3; compact and random regions):** the relative entropy of a region follows its *volume*, not its boundary: volume-only fit rms 0.0003 to 0.0011 nats against 0.0007 to 0.0015 for the Delaunay boundary alone, and with both regressors the boundary coefficient is zero or slightly negative (-0.0001 to -0.0003 against +0.0006 to +0.0013 for the volume); the response to a uniform change of couplings is extensive, so this is the expected reading and not a failure of the area law of the entropy itself. The second-order structure is confirmed (`S_rel` falls by 3.5 to 4.5 when `delta` halves, the Fisher-metric scaling) while the area ledger of the same tiles, `2 m dA(1, b)`, changes at first order, so the ratio `S_rel / dA` is not a constant: for a three-pair region it is 1.7e-2, 7.9e-3, 3.9e-3 at `delta` 0.008, 0.004, 0.002. **There is therefore no `dA = 4 G S_rel` in this model at the level of the area ledger.** The Nariai relation compares two quantities that are both quadratic in the field amplitude; here the pair's area change is linear in the bit (`+- h z`) and neutral for a pair (the two tiles change by opposite amounts), which is the structure of the fork's "zero-sum reallocation" theorem: negativity windows that integrate to zero against the modular weight. That is a conceptual match with Stage 6's negative result (a neutral dipole cannot source net area), not a derivation. **Cross-check with `brentharts/CICY`:** `pyCICY.theories.spectre` re-derives the Spectre census and areas in exact `Q(sqrt15)`, `Q(sqrt3)`; `quantumspectremodel` agrees: the swapped class of tiles at `b != 1` is the Mystic, its fraction 496/4401 matches `(5 - sqrt15)/10` to 4e-9 and `A(1, sqrt3) = 8 sqrt3`, `A(sqrt3, 1) = 10 sqrt3` (test `test_swapped_class_is_the_mystic_...`). **Not transferred:** the continuum modular theory (type III algebras, the `1/4` as a definition of `G_N`) and the Lean layer have no counterpart for 14 qubits.

### Stage 4c: can any area change in the model match a relative entropy? (**Counted**; negative)
The follow-up asked for a non-neutral area change that is quadratic in the amplitude of an excitation, the P8-displacement version closest to Nariai's setup (`relative_entropy.py`, 8 qubits of generation 3, flow time 0.05). Excitations are `exp(-i alpha sum c_q Y_q)` on a region `A` (a local unitary, so `S(rho_A)` is unchanged and `S_rel = Delta<K_A>` exactly, checked to 1e-9). What the model offers as area observables, read from the actual tiles (`geometry_area_change`, agreement 1e-9): the bump tile `A0 + h Z` and its partner `A0 - h Z` change by `+- h Delta<Z>`, **linear in the state amplitude**; their sum, the pair ledger, does not change at all; and the squared deviation `Q_A = (h sum Z)^2` is the only quadratic one. Results: (1) `S_rel` and `Delta Q_A` scale as `alpha^2` (ratios 4.0 on halving `alpha`) but the bump-tile area scales as `alpha`, so no linear area change can be proportional to `S_rel`; (2) the ratios `S_rel / Delta Q_A` are constant in `alpha` for a given region and generator but vary by region (0.51 to 0.98 for four regions of 2 to 4 pairs, with one generator each) and, across 30 random generators on one three-pair region, `S_rel / Delta Q_A` has mean 0.8 and standard deviation 14.6 (range -50 to +49): `Delta Q_A` is sign-indefinite while `S_rel >= 0`, which is the opposite of an area that grows only under positive relative entropy; `S_rel / (Delta A_bump)^2` ranges from 2.7 to 3500. So no `G_N`-like constant relates an area observable of the model to the relative entropy. (3) Why Nariai's relation works and this one does not: there `K_A` is the boost energy (Bisognano-Wichmann: the modular Hamiltonian of a wedge is a local energy weighted by position) and `S_rel = beta Delta E`; here `K_A = -ln rho_A` is **not** proportional to the region's Hamiltonian `H_A`: the best single `beta` leaves a relative residual of 0.16 (2 pairs), 0.33 (3), 0.29 (3, another region) and 0.49 (4); allowing a free weight on each term of `H_A` still leaves 0.3% (2 pairs) to 31% (4 pairs), and the weights are position dependent (`beta_eff` 3.5 to 6.5), with a few negative `ZZ` weights. This is the stage's answer: the model has the structure of the fork's zero-sum theorem (neutral ledger) but not the relation to energy that makes `S_rel` a flux integral. Not tested: mixed states / thermal references (where `S_rel = beta Delta E` is exact for unitary perturbations of the global Gibbs state, but then it says nothing about area), and regions larger than half the 8 qubits.

### Stage 4d: the first law with a thermal reference (**Counted**; positive in a limited range)
Stage 4c used the ground state, where `K_A` is not `beta H_A`. With a Gibbs reference `sigma = e^{-beta H}/Z` (flow time 0.05, `thermal_first_law`) and the same local unitary excitation: (1) for the whole system `S_rel = beta Delta<H>` exactly (ratio 1.000000, since `Delta S = 0`; checked at `beta` 0.2, 1 and 5), which is Nariai's `S_rel = beta Delta E` and contains the Coulomb energy of the area dipoles `sum K_pq Delta<Z_p Z_q>` (`K = kappa W`, `kappa` fixed by the area per bit squared) plus the tunnelling energy; (2) for a three-pair region, `K_A = beta_eff H_A + const` holds with `beta_eff = beta` and residual 0.8% at `beta` 0.05, 1.6% at 0.1, 3% at 0.2, 7.5% at 0.5, but 13% at 1, 19% at 2, 30% at 5 and 35% at 20 (where `beta_eff` saturates at 3.7), and the regional relation `S_rel(rho_A || sigma_A) = beta Delta<H_A>` holds to 1% at `beta` 0.05, 2% at 0.2, 6% at 0.5, 10% at 1, then reaches 0.83 at 5 and 0.22 at 20 (6 pairs; 8 pairs and a second region agree, 1.19 at `beta` 1 for the less central region). So the Nariai-type local first law holds in the model in the hot regime (`beta` below about 0.5 in units where `J` = 0.3 and the strongest `K` is 0.8), i.e. when correlations are short and the region's modular Hamiltonian is local, and fails as the state approaches the entangled ground state where Stage 4 measured area-like entropy. It is not a statement that the area law holds there; no area change enters, only the energy `Delta<H_A>`. Not done: connecting `Delta<H_A>` to an area change through the Coulomb term, which would need `kappa` derived from the geometry (it is a definition of `toymodel_v6`).

### Stage 4e: the modular Hamiltonian in the cold regime (**Counted**; partly positive)
Where Stage 4d's local first law fails (the ground state), what is `K_A = -ln rho_A`? `pauli_coefficients` expands it in Pauli strings (the expansion reconstructs a random Hermitian matrix to 1e-12, and for a hot Gibbs state of the region the coefficient of `X_i` over `-J` equals `beta` to 1e-9, which checks the estimator). Ground state, flow time 0.05: (1) `K_A` is mostly one- and two-body and of the same kinds as `H_A`: for four of 8 pairs, `X` carries 37% and `ZZ` 53% of its squared norm (no `Z` or `Y` at all, `XX` 0.4%); for five of 12 pairs 20% and 74%; but for five of a different 10-pair cluster only 54% is one- or two-body (46% in three- to five-body strings), so locality is not generic. (2) The weight of `X_i` is a local inverse temperature `beta_loc = c_X / (-J)`, and it grows with the depth of the pair inside the region (distance from its position to the nearest pair outside): Spearman 0.63, 0.74 and 0.80 for regions of 3, 4 and 5 of 12 pairs (30, 40, 60 points; shuffled-depth null sd 0.08), mean `beta_loc` 3.3 to 4.2, scatter about 2. (3) It does not survive to half the system: for regions of 6 of 12 pairs the correlation is 0.10 and the mean `beta_loc` is -0.28 (the `X` coefficient changes sign). **Limits:** one cluster, one flow time, 190 points, depth measured in tile-position units, no linearity in depth established (a pooled linear fit has slope 0.23 per unit and rms residual 2.7), and `ZZ` weights were not profiled. ### Stage 7: the cone-point ledger, a 2+1 reading (**Exact** for the bookkeeping; **Counted** for the Spectre sample)
Source: the ballgen paper (`paper/trionda.tex`, angle ledger of the flat hat model and of folded Spectre patches). A closed surface of flat tiles is flat except at cone points, a vertex whose tile corners add to `a` degrees has deficit `360 - a`, and the discrete Gauss-Bonnet theorem makes the deficits of a sphere add to 720 degrees. In 2+1 gravity this is the statement that point particles on a closed universe have total mass fixed by topology (Deser-Jackiw-'t Hooft); here it is a dictionary, not a derivation. `cone_ledger.py` (5 checks) computes it for three substrates:
- *Tetrahedron* (the flat model of the Trionda): 4 cone points of deficit 180 degrees, cone angle 180, i.e. the flat **pillowcase** `S^2(2,2,2,2)`; Thurston moduli dimension `n - 3 = 1`.
- *Hat Trick* (flat hats on the 92-tile geodesic sphere): the kite angles are flat everywhere except at the corners of the 12 pentagonal holes; each hole is capped by a cone point of deficit 60 degrees (cone angle 300): `12 x 60 = 720`, moduli dimension 9.
- *Closed folds of random Spectre patches* (exact DP of `spectre/dp.py`, 237 balls from patches of 4 to 21 tiles, seed 5, 80 draws per size): the ledger is 720 in all 237 and no hole remains, as the paper's proposition requires; deficits are multiples of 30 degrees and in this sample only 90, 120, 180, 240 or 270 degrees occur as non-zero deficits (never 30, 60, 150 or 210 degrees). The most common multiset is `{90 x 4, 120 x 3}` (100 of 237); the number of cone points is 4 to 8 (moduli dimension 1 to 5); no fold gives the pillowcase `{180 x 4}`. These are statements about the sample, not a classification; whether 30 and 60 degree cones are impossible for a Spectre fold is open.
Thurston's theorem (*Shapes of polyhedra*, 1998): flat cone-metrics on the sphere with `n` cone points of given positive curvatures form a complex-hyperbolic cone-manifold of complex dimension `n - 3`; used only as the dimension count in `moduli_dim`.
Pass: ledger 720 on every closed fold and a cone-point multiset for each. **Done.** Not done: the 2+1 dynamics (the roadmap's R5 asks for a source with a `1/r` potential; in 2+1 gravity there is no Newtonian force, so Stage 6's test cannot be passed in this setting).

### Stage 9a: the sphere as a phase space (**Exact** matrix identities; **Counted** convergence)
`spin_sphere.py` (5 checks). The Trionda flow `v = p x grad(xyz)` is the Hamiltonian flow of `xyz` on the sphere with its area form. Spin-`j` quantisation (2j + 1 states, `hbar_eff = 1/j`) gives `H3 = sym(Jx Jy Jz)`:
- `H3` commutes with the 12 rotations of `T` and not with a quarter-turn about `z` or with an icosahedral fifth-turn; `H6 = sum_a (a.J)^6` over the six 5-fold axes commutes with the 60 rotations of `I`. The first invariant multipoles are `l = 3` for `T` and `l = 6` for `I`, the same numbers as the seam-harmonic selection rules of the paper.
- Ehrenfest: the coherent-state expectation `<J>(t)/j` follows the classical Trionda flow (quantum time = classical time / `j^2`); the maximum error over classical time up to 1.2 is 0.085, 0.047, 0.024, 0.012 at `j` = 10, 20, 40, 80, halving as `j` doubles.
- Counting: the number of `T`-invariant states is within 1.5 of `(2j + 1)/12` and of `I`-invariant states of `(2j + 1)/60`; a region of area `A` holds about `(2j + 1) A / 4 pi` states, so each Trionda panel holds about `(2j + 1)/4`.
This is an area-to-state-counting dictionary, nothing more. **Not done:** the 12-puncture (Hat Trick) or 4-puncture (pillowcase) Hilbert space of flat `SU(2)` connections; whether the pair-qubit space embeds in it (expected to fail; a failure closes the idea, as in the decision table).

### Stage 8: do local moves change the cone data? (**Counted**, sample)
`cone_moves.py` (4 checks). The wada-coudene flip data carry no fold and the engine's placement files are not in this workspace, so the flip relation itself was not used. What was measured is the nearest thing the fold code allows: one-tile moves on closed Spectre folds (random disc patches of 5 to 19 tiles, seed 5, 40 draws per size). A move adds a frontier tile, removes a tile, or swaps one for the other, and counts only if the result is again a closed fold. 660 before/after pairs came out (add 199 of 528 tried, remove 314, swap 147).
- The ledger stays 720 degrees in all 660 pairs, as it must.
- The number of positive cone points changed in 355 pairs (54%) and the cone multiset changed in 414 (63%). The change in the count ran from -3 to +2. Swaps, which keep the tile number, changed the cone count in 81 of 147 (55%).
- Thurston's moduli dimension `n - 3` changed by the same amount, since `n` is at least 4 here.
- Reading: a boundary move can move the fold to a different moduli space. An interior planar flip leaves the patch boundary alone and so, by the cone-points-on-boundary-corners reading, leaves the cone data alone; that was **not** tested, and needs the engine's closed clusters (radius about 26).
- Verdict for kill row 7: tripped for boundary moves, undecided for interior flips. Thurston's moduli space is used per frozen patch, as the row says.

### Stage 9b: do the pair qubits fit a puncture Hilbert space? (**Exact** for the counts, **Negative** for the embedding)
`puncture_hilbert.py` (6 checks). The gauge-invariant space of a sphere with `n` spin-1/2 punctures is the singlet space of `(C^2)^n`, of dimension the Catalan number `C_{n/2}`; I built it exactly and let the tetrahedral group `T = A4` permute the punctures.
- *Dimension.* `p` pair qubits have dimension `2^p`; `2p` punctures have `C_p`. By counting, the qubits fit only for `p >= 5` (`p` = 2, 3, 4: 4 > 2, 8 > 5, 16 > 14; `p` = 5, 6: 32 < 42, 64 < 132). Counting is necessary, not an embedding, and no map was built.
- *Symmetry.* 4 punctures at a tetrahedron's vertices: 2-dimensional, `1' + 1''` of `A4` (one qubit, the `Z3` phases). 12 punctures at the Hat Trick's pentagons (icosahedron vertices, `T` acting freely): 132-dimensional, `20 * 1 + 14 * 1' + 14 * 1'' + 28 * 3` (20 + 14 + 14 + 84 = 132); 20 `T`-invariant states.
- *Structure (the failing part).* The operators on one pair that commute with SU(2) are spanned by the singlet and triplet projectors: a 2-dimensional commutative algebra. A gauge-invariant pair carries a classical bit, not a qubit with non-commuting `X` and `Z`. The singlet space also has no tensor factorisation over pairs, so `-J X_p` and `W Z_p Z_q` have no gauge-invariant local form. Pair qubits therefore do not embed as local qubits; they would need operators spanning several punctures.
- Verdict for kill row 9: tripped for local pair qubits. The spin-`j` dictionary of Stage 9a stays; the qubit network stays a code and a toy. Not done: flat connections at level `k` (the Verlinde count is smaller than `C_p`), spins other than 1/2, a non-local embedding.

### Stage 10a: the hat beyond `GP(f, 0)` (**Exact** for what is exhausted)
`gp_hat_scan.py` (4 checks; needs the `hexasphere` repository for `HatBall` and its Algorithm X). The Goldberg-Coxeter spheres `GP(m, n)`, `T = m^2 + m n + n^2`, are built from the Eisenstein lattice (counts `10T + 2` vertices, `20T` triangles, degrees 5 and 6, every edge in two faces); the kite split makes `60 (T - 1)` kites, so hats need odd `T`. For every odd `T` up to 39, both hands of every chiral pair, and the symmetry groups `I`, `T`, `C5`, `C3`, `C2`, `C1` (hat hand fixed) and `C1` with both hands: **no hat tiling with 12 pentagon holes exists except on `GP(3,0)` = `GP(0,3)`** (`T = 9`, the Hat Trick: 2 tilings with `I`, 2 with `T`, 16 with `C5` or `C3`, 64 with `C2`, at least 1000 with `C1`). All searches other than the `GP(3,0)` `C1` ones ended exhausted with zero tilings (`T = 3` has no hat placement at all; every kite is covered by some placement for `T >= 7`). This extends the earlier `GP(f,0)` scan to the chiral classes. It is not a proof for larger `T`, for other hole patterns, or for tilings with holes of another kind.
Consequence for Stage 5: a hat (aperiodic) structure does not continue to larger closed spheres by Goldberg-Coxeter subdivision, so the scale ladder on a closed curved space cannot be the planar inflation; the hat sphere is a single isolated size.

### Stage 4g — negative temperature (β < 0) and the thermal first law

**Counted** (`relative_entropy.thermal_first_law`, region (0,1,2) of 6 pairs, flow 0.05; test `test_local_first_law_is_controlled_by_abs_beta_not_by_the_sign_of_the_temperature`):
- The global first law S_rel = βΔ⟨H⟩ holds exactly for either sign of β (ratio 1.0000 for β from −5 to 5).
- The regional law K_A ≈ β_eff·H_A has the same locality residual for +β and −β (0.008, 0.032, 0.084 at |β| = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5; 0.370 and 0.346 at |β| = 20) and a regional ratio that deviates from 1 with opposite sign.

**Not found**: negative temperature does not rescue the first law at large |β|. At β = −5 the regional ratio is 0.59 and the residual 0.33. The "first law holds only when hot" idea is supported; "negative kelvin" adds nothing here, because H is bounded, so −H is a mirror model.

### Stage 4h — S⁷→S⁴ on pairs of pair-qubits, and tier-to-tier entanglement without bridges (`hopf_pairs.py`, 7 tests)

**Exact.** Write a two-qubit state (a, b, c, d) as q1 = a + bj, q2 = c + dj. The quaternionic Hopf map of the HopfFibration fork, (2 q1 q̄2, |q1|²−|q2|²), then has (x1, x2, x5) equal to the Bloch vector of qubit 1, and (x3, x4) of length C = 2|ad−bc|, the concurrence. So |r₁|² + C² = 1. The fibre (right multiplication) is a local SU(2) on qubit 2 and does not move the base point. S⁴ is "qubit 1 plus how entangled it is". Concurrence is *not* a function of x5 alone (a search for C = √(1−x5²) fails).

**Counted** (central pairs of generation 3, flow ground state). For a mixed pair of pair-qubits the map is undefined, but the leak D = (1−|r_p|²) − C² ≥ 0 measures how far the pair is from the pure two-qubit picture (D = 0 exactly when it is pure).
- 2 pairs: D = 0, so the S⁷→S⁴ picture is exact.
- 8 pairs, strongest-coupled partners, mean D: about 0.68 at flow 0.05 and about 0.06 at flow 0.2.
- 12 pairs: D 0.82 at flow 0.05, 0.11 at flow 0.2.
- So the picture applies only on the hot side of the flow, where the entanglement is mostly pairwise. In the cold cat regime nearly all of it is shared with the rest.

**SUPERSEDED by Stage 4i.** The statement below that the exact patches do not nest was WRONG (my test allowed only translations and 30-degree rotations and missed the slot transform); the overlay is kept only as a control. **Counted, model-dependent (overlay, superseded).** Using the same dipole law between overlaid tiers (4 pairs per tier, generations 2, 3, 4):
- The ground state at flow ≤ 0.13 is the parity cat, and every tier pair has mutual information ln 2 = 0.693. That is GHZ-type parity correlation, not tier entanglement. With the doublet resolved into one classical pattern, tier mutual information is ≤ 0.0013 nats at flow 0.05.
- Real tier entanglement appears at flow 0.3 and 0.6. With the centred overlay it is 0.94, 0.56, 0.49 nats for (1,2), (2,3), (1,3) at flow 0.3. With the inflation-scaled overlay it is 0.05, 0.54, 0.03. The "nearest tier is most entangled" ordering therefore depends on the overlay choice and is not robust.
- Exact quench from |0…0⟩ with no bridges: tier mutual information 0.003–0.03 nats up to t = 16.
- The hand-chosen CX/CZ bridges give 0.21, 0.42, 0.26 nats after 3 cycles. Without bridges the network state is an exact product (0.000).
- Random offsets of the tiers at SD 3 and 10 give entanglement in every sample, so it does not need the Spectre geometry; the overlay (dipoles landing on top of each other, cross couplings up to 55× the intra-tier ones) drives it.

**Verdict (superseded).** See Stage 4i: the tiers nest exactly, so no overlay is needed.


### Stage 4i — the tiers nest exactly; tier-to-tier is region-to-region (`nested_tiers.py`, 3 tests); correction of Stage 4h

**Correction.** Stage 4h said the exact patches of different generations "do not nest at one scale". That was wrong. My test looked for a translation and a rotation by a multiple of 30° and missed the substitution's slot transform.

**Exact** (found reading the `ray` fork, whose horned-sphere ring tree is a recursive parent–child object): `spectre_exact.build` makes the level-k patch of species S the union, over eight slots, of the level-(k−1) patches of the child species `SUPER_RULES[S][idx]`, each moved by `Ts[idx]`. For the single-tile species (Delta), slot 1 is Delta again, so as sets of exact leaf tiles patch(k) = Ts[1]·patch(k−1) for k = 2, 3, 4: 9 ⊂ 71 ⊂ 559 ⊂ 4401 tiles, verified by exact key comparison. Every pair of patch(k) is also a pair of patch(k+1): 6 ⊂ 82 ⊂ 804. The Mystic species (Gamma, slot 7) nests the same way (8 ⊂ 62 ⊂ 488 ⊂ 3842). So the three tiers of `TriSpectreNetwork` are nested regions of one tiling, not three systems.

**Counted** (one cluster of 16 pair qubits: the 6 pairs of tier 2, then the 5 nearest pairs new in tier 3, then the 5 nearest new in tier 4; the dipole matrix W is the real one of patch(4); no overlay, no bridges; symmetry-broken ground state to remove the ln 2 of the parity cat; flow times 0.13 and 0.2):
- Mutual information I(tier 2 : tier 3) = 0.47, 0.56 nats; I(tier 3 : tier 4) = 0.03, 0.08; I(tier 2 : tier 4) = 0.35, 0.35. The entanglement across the nesting boundaries is not zero, and it is not ordered "previous and next only": tiers 2 and 4 are more entangled than 3 and 4.
- Against the same pairs assigned to the shells at random (12 shuffles), the nested shells are not more entangled: I(tier 2 : tier 3) is 0.47 against 0.50 at flow 0.13 and 0.56 against 0.55 at 0.2; for shells (6, 10) it is 0.62 and 0.71 against 1.16 ± 0.36 and 1.12 ± 0.24; for (6, 6, 4) 0.50 and 0.59 against 0.61 ± 0.17 and 0.70 ± 0.15. Compact shells have a small boundary, so what the nesting adds is geometry, as in the area-versus-volume tests of Stage 4e.
- At flow time 0.05 the mutual information is at most 0.014 nats; at 0.3 and 0.6 tiers 2:3 give 0.38 and 0.26.

**Verdict.** "Each iteration entangled with the previous and next" has a real, exact setting: the nesting 6 ⊂ 82 ⊂ 804. In it the entanglement between iterations is ordinary region entanglement of one tiling, set by proximity, and the hierarchy by itself gives no more of it than a random grouping. One cluster size and two shell layouts; whether the hierarchy matters at scale needs the 82- and 804-pair states, which Stage 4 could not reach.

**Ray fork, Möbius dictionary (not implemented as code).** The Möbius invariants of a pair of Hopf fibres over Bloch points a, b are s1 = s2 = |cos(θ_ab/2)| = |⟨a|b⟩|: the fibre pair is a "Hopf clasp" exactly when the states are orthogonal, which is the horned-sphere clasp at κ = 1 + 1/√2 (s1 = 0). The two bit values of a pair qubit, the poles of the Bloch sphere, are such a pair. All fibres of one Hopf fibration link pairwise once, whatever the state, so the linking graph carries no information about entanglement; the invariants carry the overlap, which the Bloch-vector tools already give. The ground state is real, so the Bloch vectors of a pair qubit lie in the xz great circle, and the fibres on the Clifford torus. Nothing new for the model came from this beyond the nesting above.
